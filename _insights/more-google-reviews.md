# How to get more Google reviews without being pushy

<div class="insight-meta" style="margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
  <span>5 min read</span>
  <span class="dot"></span>
  <span>Updated August 2026</span>

The businesses with the most Google reviews aren't necessarily better at asking, they just ask at the right moment and make it effortless when they do. 97% of people read reviews before choosing a local business, and the trust sweet spot sits around 4.2 to 4.5 stars, not a perfect five. Getting there consistently is less about being pushy and more about asking the right customer at the right moment, every time, without relying on memory.

## Why reviews matter more than most businesses realize

Reviews are a real input into local search ranking, not just a trust signal for visitors. They're also a moving target: 73% of people say they only trust reviews from the last 30 days, so a strong total count from two years ago carries less weight than a steady, recent trickle. Businesses that display reviews prominently see a meaningful lift in conversion, and that effect grows with the number of recent reviews, not just the total.

## The two things that kill review requests

The first is timing: asking days after the interaction, once the good feeling has faded, gets a much lower response rate than asking right away. The second is asking everyone the same way regardless of how the interaction actually went, which produces low response rates at best, and at worst invites a review from someone who wasn't actually happy.

## When to ask (and when not to)

The best moment is shortly after a clearly positive interaction, while it's still fresh. If there were complaints or friction along the way, that's the moment to hold off, or to reach out privately instead. This is exactly where a bit of automation helps: a request can trigger off a completed, positive interaction rather than going out to every contact by default.

## How to respond to the ones that come in

97% of people read how a business responds to its reviews, not just the star rating. Thank positive reviewers specifically, referencing something real about the interaction rather than a copy-pasted line. Respond to negative ones calmly and specifically, and never argue in public. Responsiveness matters beyond reviews too: businesses that reply to inquiries within 24 hours see roughly 50% better conversion from inquiry to sale, which is the same instinct at work.

## Keeping it consistent without becoming a chore

This is where most businesses fall off, not from lack of care, but because remembering to ask every single time is genuinely hard alongside everything else you're doing. Automating the request so it goes out right after a job or booking closes, and flagging new reviews with a drafted response ready for your approval, keeps the habit going without it living on your to-do list.
